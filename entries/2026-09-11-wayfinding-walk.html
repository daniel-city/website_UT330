Interaction Field Study Assignment

September 24, 2026 · Week 4

I collected parking-related data at the Amtrak Station in Ann Arbor, MI to better understand the wayfinding infrastructure available to better understand where parking is, the cost, and how to get from the parking lot to the Amtrak Station. I chose this topic from a recent trip to the Argo Cascades, next to the Amtrak Station.

Introduction

During this past week in UT330, I worked on the Interaction Field Study Assignment. For this assignment, I collected data from a specific location/system in Ann Arbor and analyzed wayfinding, decision points, and the clarity of information being shown.

For this project, I decided to focus on the Amtrak Station in Ann Arbor, near the Argo Cascades. The reason why I decided to focus on this was because, on a trip to the Argo Cascades a few weeks ago, I accidentally tried to enter the Cascades from the North parking lot at the Amtrak Station but didn’t realize it didn’t connect to the Cascades. From this, I realized that there are also many decision points for parking at the Amtrak Station, and I wanted to investigate what information is being shown to help with navigation.

Data Collection

In terms of data collection, I took photos of a variety of elements. One of the elements I took photos of were signs and infrastructure explaining the cost for parking, parking meters, and signs indicating parking. I also took photos that encapsulated more of my surroundings, to show more of the surroundings. I also included a screenshot of the Amtrak website, to show how much information is shown on the website regarding parking availability options.

Mapping Data

To display the photos I took, I decided to make a Google My Maps to outline the path I took and where I took photos. I decided to do this because my field study was particularly a very spatial one; one that needs to consider spatially how someone were to move around the space and get around.

Key ideas

My main takeaway from this field study was that there could be more signage to help with wayfinding to and from the North parking lot. For one, it would help if there was a parking sign at the front of the Amtrak Station to guide people to the North Parking Lot if the one in front of the Amtrak station is full. Then, after parking, there could be more signs guiding people to the station, as it isn’t immediately clear what path to take, and what accessible paths there are.

Summary

Overall, this field study project has encouraged me to think more about all of the different wayfinding sign categories that need to be considered: wayfinding while driving, wayfinding while walking, accessibility, costs, etc.
